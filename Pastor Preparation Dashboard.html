Method

How to use this

Do not binge content. For each module:

1. Watch
Learn from an experienced teacher or pastor.
2. Read
Slow down and engage the argument.
3. Study Scripture
Test the teaching against the biblical text.
4. Practice
Turn knowledge into pastoral competence.
Priority

Your formation order

Build depth before complexity. The recommended order is:

Scripture & doctrine → character & prayer → preaching → pastoral care → leadership → administration → church planting.

A future pastor is not ready because he has consumed a large library. Readiness grows through tested character, doctrinal stability, accountable service, repeated ministry practice, and trusted leaders affirming his fitness.
Track 1

Bible, Hermeneutics & Theology

Module 1 · Biblical Story

Understand the Bible as one unified story

Watch / Course: BibleProject Classroom — choose a foundational class on the Hebrew Bible, biblical story, or interpretation.

Read: Genesis 1–3; Genesis 12; Exodus 19; 2 Samuel 7; Jeremiah 31; Luke 24; Acts 2; Revelation 21–22.

Practice: Write a one-page biblical-theology overview: Creation → Fall → Covenant → Christ → Church → New Creation.

Module 2 · Hermeneutics

Learn to interpret before you apply

Watch: Use BibleProject Classroom's interpretation material and compare how genre, literary context, historical setting, and canonical context affect meaning.

Read: Nehemiah 8:1–8; Luke 24:25–27; Acts 17:10–12; 2 Timothy 2:15.

Practice: Choose one paragraph from an epistle. Identify author, audience, context, structure, main point, theological claims, and legitimate applications.

Module 3 · Doctrine

Build a pastor-level doctrinal framework

Study: Work systematically through Scripture, Trinity, Christology, humanity and sin, salvation, sanctification, Holy Spirit, church, ordinances, gifts, last things, and final judgment.

Practice: For every doctrine, maintain four sections: biblical texts, doctrinal summary, major evangelical disagreements, and pastoral implications.

Preaching & Sermon Training

Sermon Lab 1 · Observation

Listen like a preacher, not merely a listener

Each week, listen to one full sermon from a respected expositor. Do not copy style. Analyze structure, handling of the text, theological claims, illustrations, gospel connection, application, transitions, and conclusion.

Leadership/preaching library: Desiring God — Church Leadership Messages.

Sermon Lab 2 · Short Exposition

5-minute sermons

Prepare and record 12 five-minute expositions. Each must have one main point, faithful explanation, one clear application, and a concise conclusion.

20-minute sermon cycle

Prepare 12 full sermons from different genres: Torah, narrative, wisdom, psalm, prophet, Gospel, Acts, Pauline epistle, general epistle, apocalyptic, topical-doctrinal, and evangelistic.

After each sermon: get feedback from a mature Christian leader and write three strengths, three weaknesses, and one concrete change for the next sermon.

Plan an entire series

Choose one short biblical book. Produce the series title, purpose, passage divisions, sermon main ideas, theological themes, applications, and final sermon manuscripts.

Pastoral Case Lab

Real-World Ministry Judgment

Work through these with an experienced pastor. The objective is judgment, not merely getting the “right answer.”

Case 1 — Volunteer Conflict

Two key volunteers accuse each other of disrespect and threaten to leave. Develop your fact-finding, reconciliation, documentation, follow-up, and escalation plan.

Case 2 — Doctrinal Disagreement

A ministry leader begins publicly teaching something outside the church's Statement of Faith. Determine how you would distinguish secondary disagreement from disqualifying doctrinal conflict.

Case 3 — Financial Concern

A staff member repeatedly submits unclear reimbursements. Map the controls, conversation, review, documentation, and board involvement required.

Case 4 — Pastoral Crisis

A congregant reports an urgent mental-health or safety concern. Identify immediate safety actions, pastoral role boundaries, emergency/professional referral, documentation, confidentiality limits, and follow-up.

Case 5 — Staff Underperformance

A beloved ministry leader has strong relationships but repeatedly fails core responsibilities. Create a coaching, expectations, review, improvement, and possible transition process.

Case 6 — Rapid Growth

Attendance doubles quickly. Determine what must change first in children’s safety, volunteer systems, pastoral care, finances, communications, facilities, leadership span, and discipleship.

Bible Mastery

Whole-Bible Pastor Track

Before treating commentary writing or theological systems as a substitute for direct biblical familiarity, work through the canonical text itself.

Pass 1 — Read

Read all 66 books straight through. Record only one sentence per chapter so note-taking does not stop momentum.

Pass 2 — Outline

Create a one-page outline for every biblical book: purpose, structure, major themes, key texts, Christ/gospel connections, and pastoral uses.

Pass 3 — Teach

Teach or record a 10-minute overview of every book. Explain what it says, why it matters, and how it fits the biblical story.

Mentorship

Pastoral Mentorship System

Meet regularly with a mature pastor or ministry leader. Bring actual questions, failures, ministry situations, sermons, and decisions—not merely theoretical theology.

Quarterly review: character · doctrine · Bible knowledge · prayer · preaching · people skills · conflict · reliability · leadership · humility · emotional maturity · family/relationships · finances · sexual integrity · teachability.

Annual question: “What would make you hesitant to recommend me for pastoral ministry today?” Record the answer and build your next development goals around it.

Three-Year Curriculum

Pastor Preparation Sequence

A deliberate order: Bible and character first, then preaching and shepherding, then leadership and church-plant readiness. Complete this alongside your local-church service and college responsibilities rather than treating it as a race.

Year 1 · Biblical & Personal Foundations

Build the pastor before building the organization

Quarter 1: BibleProject Introduction to the Hebrew Bible; read Genesis–Deuteronomy; study 1 Timothy; begin a prayer journal; deliver four 5-minute expositions.

Quarter 2: Continue whole-Bible reading; study hermeneutics and biblical theology; survey the early Church through the Reformation; read 2 Timothy and Titus repeatedly; begin monthly mentor reviews.

Quarter 3: Complete Basics of Preaching; study systematic theology foundations and modern Church history; analyze 12 sermons; serve consistently in one local-church ministry without adding unnecessary titles.

Quarter 4: Complete The Work of the Spirit; write a biblical theology of the Holy Spirit and gifts; finish the first whole-Bible reading pass; preach/teach at least four supervised messages if opportunities are provided.

Year 1 outcome: Whole Bible read once, basic hermeneutical competence, a broad Church-history survey, stable devotional rhythms, clearer doctrine, 12+ short teaching reps, 12 sermon analyses, and documented mentor feedback.

Year 2 · Pastoral Competence

Move from student to supervised practitioner

Quarter 1: The Pastor as Shepherd; shepherding-text study; supervised pastoral visits; begin pastoral case lab.

Quarter 2: Christ-Centered Preaching; prepare full sermon manuscripts; preach across different biblical genres; receive written feedback.

Quarter 3: Basics/Restoring Biblical Eldership and Doctrine of the Church; study governance, membership, discipline, ordinances, elders, deacons, and church unity; complete a focused study of Pentecostal and charismatic history.

Quarter 4: Church Administration; mock budgets, internal controls, hiring workflows, volunteer systems, safeguarding, emergency response, and staff/elder meeting simulations.

Year 2 outcome: 12 full sermons, pastoral-care exposure, governance competence, Pentecostal and charismatic historical literacy, financial/administrative literacy, conflict simulations, and a formal annual pastoral evaluation.

Year 3 · Leadership & Multiplication

Learn to lead leaders without rushing into senior leadership

Quarter 1: Developing Leaders; disciple and develop people under church oversight; practice delegation, feedback, and volunteer leadership.

Quarter 2: Advanced preaching: preach a short-book series; complete the 48-sermon analysis challenge; teach a doctrine series.

Quarter 3: Church-planting courses; community exegesis; launch-team theory; financial runway; staffing stages; discipleship pathway; evangelism strategy.

Quarter 4: Awaken Life readiness dossier and external assessment. Ask mature pastors to identify reasons you should wait, not merely reasons you could launch.

Year 3 outcome: demonstrated leadership of people, leader-development experience, sustained preaching competence, church-operations understanding, and an evidence-based assessment of what still needs years of development.

Weekly Rhythm

Normal Training Week

Monday — Bible: 45–60 minutes of direct Scripture reading and notes.

Tuesday — Class: 30–60 minutes of a structured theology/ministry course.

Wednesday — Preaching: exegete a passage or work on a sermon.

Thursday — Leadership: read leadership/pastoral material or complete a case study.

Friday — Formation: prayer, reflection, character review, and lighter reading.

Saturday — Review: finish incomplete work, analyze a sermon, or meet with a mentor.

Sunday — Church: worship, serve, observe pastoral leadership, and record one lesson learned from actual ministry.

During a 10-unit college semester, consistency matters more than volume. A sustainable 4–6 hours of intentional pastoral preparation per week is more valuable than repeatedly designing schedules you cannot maintain.
